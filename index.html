    How the staleness clock works

    Every two minutes the app reads posted waits for both Disneyland and California Adventure through ThemeParks.wiki and notes when each ride's posted number last changed. Both parks are tracked whichever one is on screen, so switching parks doesn't reset the clock.

    While the app has been open for a while, “unchanged” is measured directly. Right after you open it, the app falls back to the feed's own update time, which moves whenever anything about the ride changes, so those figures are a minimum and show as “at least”.

    With the posted wait logger connected, the app reads when each number last changed from the logger, which checks every 5 minutes whether or not the app is open. Those times are exact (to within about 5 minutes) right away.

    A green arrow marks a ride that reopened after being down. People leave the line when a ride goes down, so for a short while after it reopens the real line is often shorter than the posted wait. That flag lasts 20 minutes by default (List → Flag settings).

    A blue down arrow on a pin means the posted wait is likely to drop at its next update. It needs the posted wait logger, which tallies each ride's posted waits through the day and counts four signs: the wait is well above that ride's average so far today, it's the ride's highest so far today, it just jumped 10 or more minutes, or it was posted after 8 PM. Rides with 3 or more signs get the arrow. In the first week of logs, the next posted change after that was a drop about 3 times in 4, usually by 5 to 10 minutes. It predicts the posted number, not your actual wait, and it's switched off during California Adventure's after-hours event nights.

    A stale posted wait is a hunch, not a promise. Timing your own waits here builds the evidence either way.

    Stopwatch rules (timing protocol)

    These follow Disney's retired red-card method, which handed out cards at the queue entrance and collected them at boarding, so every run is timed the same way.

    1. Start the moment you take your place at the back of the standby line. If the line is inside the entrance, that is the standby entrance by the posted wait sign. If it spills outside, it is the back of that extended line.
    2. Lap at the sign: if you started outside the entrance, tap “Passed the posted wait sign” as you pass it. That gives both the true wait and the red-card-style wait.
    3. Stop the moment you step into the ride vehicle, boat, or cabin. Not when you reach the load area, and not when the ride starts moving.
    4. Preshows count. Keep the clock running and tap the preshow lap buttons (Haunted Mansion, Rise of the Resistance, Smugglers Run, Runaway Railway, WEB SLINGERS, Guardians of the Galaxy) so preshow time can be separated later.
    5. Logged automatically at start: the posted wait, the time of day, and how long that posted wait had gone unchanged.
    6. Don't use the run if you used Lightning Lane, single rider, or rider swap. If the ride breaks down while you're in line, keep timing and tick the “ride went down” box.

    Not timed: showtime attractions (Enchanted Tiki Room, Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln, Mickey's PhilharMagic, Turtle Talk with Crush, Animation Academy), walk-throughs with no line (including Sorcerer's Workshop, The Bakery Tour and Redwood Creek Challenge Trail) and the Games of Pixar Pier. Riverboat and Columbia waits follow departure times, so expect jumpy numbers. Each ride's own stop point shows when you tap it on the map.
